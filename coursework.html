  Professor: Andrew Chong           Grade: In Progress

The most fundamental result in Euclidean convex analysis, the Krein-Milman theorem, essentially states "in a convex set, every point is a convex combination of the extreme points". We will explore various analogous notions of convexity when the underlying Euclidean space is instead replaced by a set of measures. Some results we will cover include the classical De Finetti theorem for exchangeable sequences, Birkhoff's theorem for doubly stochastic matrices, Dirichlet processes, ergodic theory, Berstein's theorem characterizing the class of moment generating functions, and more! 

  Professor: Steve Evans           Grade: A

Stochastic evolutions are widespread in probability, economics, statistics, and theoretical physics, and are commonly used to model various real-life phenomena. This class focuses on non-stationary evolutions in which the variance grows in time. We discuss both classical approaches, based on approximations with Brownian motions and Ito integrals, and modern high-dimensional settings, where objects of random matrix theory become relevant.

Convex optimization is a class of nonlinear optimization problems where the objective to be minimized, and the constraints, are both convex. The course covers some convex optimization theory and algorithms, and describes various applications arising in engineering design, machine learning and statistics, finance, and operations research. The course includes laboratory assignments, which consist of hands-on experiments with the optimization software CVX, and a discussion section. We used Boyd’s Convex Optimization text, covering chapters 1-7 and 11
